## Limits, before any number

Synthetic attacks on replayed data are not real adversaries. An attacker who plants the same fake across several declared sources defeats corroboration counting, and this run demonstrates exactly that. The TEE proves fetch, not truth, and in this build there is no attestation at all: every receipt carries `attested: false`. Independence groups are a judgement call made by the policy owner, and they are public on chain, so an attacker knows which sources to aim at. Paper only. No return is claimed for any desk or for Krexa.

**This run used scripted Analysts, not language models.** The Analyst is a rule that keys on two credibility cues, and the attack templates carry those same cues by construction, so a difference between the desks partly measures our own regex. We wrote both the attack and the defence. That is the weakest form of evidence available and no claim here survives without the model runs.

The window above is far too short to show trading skill. The skill verdict for every desk is `CANT_TELL_YET`, and SPEC.md 12 works through the arithmetic.

## Source reachability, and why it limits what the next section can say

A refusal for `R_UNCORROBORATED` means one of two different things. With every price group present it means the independent groups disagreed, which is the gate doing its job. With groups missing it may only mean a venue was unreachable from the machine that recorded the archive. Those are not the same finding and this run does not average them silently.

| price groups reachable | decision points |
|---|---|
| 4 | 156 |
| 3 | 128 |
| 2 | 37 |
| 1 | 1 |
| 0 | 1 |

Only 156 of 323 points (48%) had all 4 groups. On the rest, the highest k the gate could possibly have reached was lower than the policy's size table allows for, so some refusals below are a property of the data rather than of the evidence.
