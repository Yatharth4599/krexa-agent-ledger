## Limits, before any number

Synthetic attacks on replayed data are not real adversaries. An attacker who plants the same fake across several declared sources defeats corroboration counting, and this run demonstrates exactly that. The TEE proves fetch, not truth, and in this build there is no attestation at all: every receipt carries `attested: false`. Independence groups are a judgement call made by the policy owner, and they are public on chain, so an attacker knows which sources to aim at. Paper only. No return is claimed for any desk or for Krexa.

**This run used scripted Analysts, not language models.** The Analyst is a rule that keys on two credibility cues, and the attack templates carry those same cues by construction, so a difference between the desks partly measures our own regex. We wrote both the attack and the defence. That is the weakest form of evidence available and no claim here survives without the model runs.

**hier submitted no intents at all.** A desk that never trades cannot be attacked, so its zero is not evidence of a defence. Read those rows as missing data.

About 8 days of trading data cannot show trading skill. The skill verdict for every desk is `CANT_TELL_YET`, and SPEC.md 12 works through the arithmetic.

## The cost of the defence on clean runs

**Not measurable from this run, for a different reason.** There were 36 clean decisions, and every one of them was a HOLD. No desk submitted a single clean-run intent, so there is nothing for the gate to have blocked and the block rate is undefined rather than zero.

Why: the scripted Analyst's only clean-run signal is 24 hour price momentum crossing a threshold, and 24 hour momentum barely moves across a window this short, so it is close to constant within the run. With no news source enabled either, a clean point carries nothing that would make the Analyst act. The clean-run cost therefore needs a window long enough for momentum to actually vary, which the growing archive will provide, or a news feed, which is held up on terms (see data/archive/SOURCES.md).

This matters more than it looks. The clean-run cost is the half of the research question that could make the whole answer negative: a gate that blocks fakes and also blocks every profitable early signal is not worth deploying. Reporting an attack result without it would be reporting only the flattering half.
